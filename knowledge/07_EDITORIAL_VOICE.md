# Editorial Voice

QING YUN JIAN editorial content uses British English and should feel calm, refined, clear, culturally respectful, modern, warm, specific, restrained, and accessible to Singapore readers.

## Writing principles

- Prefer concrete nouns and precise verbs over decorative adjectives.
- Explain cultural references with context and humility.
- Separate verified tradition from contemporary brand interpretation.
- Use warmth without false intimacy.
- Vary structure naturally; do not repeat programmatic section patterns.
- Make every paragraph useful. Never pad to reach a word count.
- Use short sentences where clarity benefits, not as a repetitive stylistic effect.

## Approved phrases

- Born to Ascend
- Sparkling Tea Reimagined
- Modern Oriental tea
- Thoughtfully crafted
- Inspired by Oriental tea culture
- A contemporary expression of tea

Approved phrases must still fit the factual context. “Thoughtfully crafted” cannot substitute for product evidence.

## Prohibited or restricted language

Avoid exaggerated luxury language, repetitive poetic filler, generic AI transitions, excessive sentence fragments, unsupported superlatives, keyword stuffing, fake founder quotations, and unnecessarily academic prose.

Restricted unless objectively substantiated and approved:

- Best
- Number one
- Healthiest
- Cure
- Prevent
- Detox
- Boost immunity
- Guaranteed
- Miracle
- Guilt-free
- Zero risk

## Examples

Approved: “This contemporary drink pairs jasmine tea with fruit and carbonation.”

Not approved: “A revolutionary miracle elixir that detoxes the body and elevates every moment.”

Approved: “The recipe and sweetness level can affect the final nutritional profile.”

Not approved: “A guilt-free alternative that is always healthier than bubble tea.”

Approved: “The founder describes the decision in the approved interview dated [date].”

Not approved: An unattributed or fabricated founder quotation.
