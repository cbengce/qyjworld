# Legal and Claims Standard

This checklist manages editorial risk; it is not legal or medical advice.

## Claims checklist

- **Health claims:** Is the effect supported by appropriate evidence and within the approved scope?
- **Nutritional comparisons:** Are products, serving sizes, recipes, and data sources genuinely comparable?
- **Disease claims:** Remove claims to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent disease unless specifically authorised after qualified and legal review.
- **Medical advice:** Do not personalise medical guidance or replace qualified care.
- **Investment language:** Do not publish offers, guaranteed returns, projections, or implied financial participation without Singapore legal review.
- **Testimonials:** Confirm authenticity, consent, context, and whether typicality or disclosure is required.
- **Rankings:** Require a transparent, current methodology. Never claim “number one” or “best” without objective substantiation.
- **Competitor comparisons:** Compare like with like using current evidence; avoid denigration.
- **Student-related claims:** Do not imply tea replaces sleep, nutrition, hydration, movement, or professional support.
- **Cultural and historical accuracy:** Use authoritative sources and distinguish tradition from modern interpretation.
- **Allergens and ingredients:** Match the current approved recipe and allergen record.
- **Price and promotion accuracy:** Confirm currency, dates, eligibility, availability, terms, and approval immediately before publication.

## Qualified review required

- Medical content
- Nutritional advice
- Caffeine guidance
- Pregnancy guidance
- Investment-facing material
- Regulated claims

## Cautious language

Appropriate phrases may include “may”, “can vary”, “depends on the recipe”, “current evidence suggests”, and “consult a qualified professional where appropriate”. Cautious wording alone does not make an unsupported claim acceptable. Evidence and scope are still required.

## Publication gate

Record the exact claim, evidence, reviewer, review date, permitted wording, and expiry or re-check date. If evidence is absent or the required reviewer has not approved it, the claim does not publish.
